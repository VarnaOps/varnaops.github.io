Vision

VarnaOps is building the reference layer for single-cell biology.

Reference-mapped, not de novo

Most automated annotation labels a dataset on its own terms — clustering it, then guessing what each cluster is. We take the opposite approach: every cell is mapped onto a curated reference atlas, so its label, its neighbours, and its confidence all come from a consistent, biologically-grounded frame rather than from one dataset's noise.

A reference factory

The hard, durable work in single-cell isn't any one model — it's building clean, harmonized, well-licensed reference atlases. VarnaOps is designed as a factory for those references: the same engine, applied organ by organ. Human brain is live; immune is in progress; more organs follow. Each new reference makes the whole platform more useful without changing how it's used.

Healthy backbone, disease per query

Our references are healthy backbones — broad, well-sampled maps of normal tissue. Disease isn't baked into the reference; it's layered in per analysis, where your own cohort is compared against that stable healthy baseline. That keeps the reference reusable across conditions instead of locking it to one study or one disease.

Confidence, and the unknown

A label without a confidence is a liability. Every prediction carries a per-cell confidence score, and cells that don't fit the reference are flagged as deviant rather than forced into the nearest known type. Being honest about what the reference hasn't seen is what makes the calls trustworthy.

Standardized biology

Labels are aligned to the Cell Ontology, so results are comparable across studies, organs, and time — not a private vocabulary that only makes sense inside one report.

Built to scale

The pipeline is designed to hold up from a few thousand cells to atlas scale on modest hardware, so the same workflow serves a single experiment and a whole-tissue map.
